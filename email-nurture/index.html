Hi {First name},

A comeback costs more than the repeat labour. The bay is blocked, a paying job waits, a senior technician stops to diagnose, and the customer starts wondering whether to come back at all.

Try this with your own numbers: comebacks per week × average hours to redo × your hourly bay rate. Then add the jobs you could not take while that bay was busy. For most workshops, the total is larger than the training budget.

Rules that run the sequence

Who gets what, and when they leave

Segmentation

Two tracks, one sequence

  • Service and workshop heads (60% of leads): throughput, comebacks, technician time.
  • Dealer principals and GMs: service revenue, retention and payback.
  • Same six steps, different subject lines and proof points.
MQL scoring

When a lead becomes an MQL

  • Uses the ROI calculator, or
  • Clicks in 2 or more emails, or
  • Visits the pricing or demo page.
  • Leads that score are handed to sales within 24 hours.
Exit and fallback

Stop selling once they say yes

  • Booking a demo removes the lead from the sequence at once.
  • Non-openers of email 6 get one resend with a new subject.
  • No engagement after 21 days: move to a monthly newsletter.
